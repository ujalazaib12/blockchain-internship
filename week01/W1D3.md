Day 3 Task: Consensus & The Network
Nodes
A node is just a computer that is part of the blockchain network. Every node keeps a copy of the entire ledger and helps check that new transactions and blocks follow the rules. More nodes means the network is more spread out and harder to control or attack.

Miners vs Validators:
Miners and validators are the people who create new blocks, but they do it in different ways.

Miners use heavy computing power to solve a difficult math puzzle. Whoever solves it first gets to add the next block and earns a reward. This is called Proof of Work. It uses a lot of electricity because thousands of computers are racing to solve the puzzle at once.

Validators don't compete with computing power. Instead they lock up some of their own coins as a deposit, called staking. The network then picks a validator to create the next block, usually based on how much they staked. This is called Proof of Stake. It uses far less energy because there is no puzzle solving race

Proof of Work vs Proof of Stake:
Both are just different methods to decide who gets to add the next block and to stop people from cheating. Proof of Work relies on spending real world energy and computing power. Proof of Stake relies on people risking their own money, since if a validator acts dishonestly, their staked coins can be taken away as a penalty.

What is a Fork:
A fork happens when the blockchain splits into two paths. This can happen for two reasons. Sometimes it is temporary and accidental, like when two miners find a block at almost the same time and the network briefly has two versions until one gets accepted. Other times it is intentional, when the community changes the rules of the blockchain. If the change is small and still compatible with old rules, it is called a soft fork. If the change is big and creates a completely separate version of the chain going forward, it is called a hard fork.

Bitcoin vs Ethereum at a High Level:
Bitcoin was built mainly to be digital money, a way to store and send value without a bank. It uses Proof of Work.
Ethereum was built to be more than money. It runs smart contracts, which are small programs that live on the blockchain and can automate agreements, apps, and even other tokens. Ethereum used to run on Proof of Work too, but it switched to Proof of Stake to save energy and increase speed.
